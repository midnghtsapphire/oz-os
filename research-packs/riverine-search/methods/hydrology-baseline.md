# Method: Hydrology Baseline Assessment

**Domain:** Environmental Science / Hydrology
**Method type:** Industry-standard

## How It Works
Before deploying any search technology, establish the hydrological baseline of the
river system: flow rate, depth profile, seasonal variation, known hazards (dams,
rapids, logjams), and historical water level data. This determines which search
methods are even feasible and where to concentrate effort.

## Why It Works
A river is not a static search area. Water moves objects downstream at variable
rates. Without understanding the hydrology, teams search the wrong stretch of river.
USGS stream gauge data is free and available for most US waterways.

## Why It Fails
- Gauge data may not exist for small tributaries or remote waterways
- Seasonal variation means historical data may not reflect current conditions
- Does not account for artificial changes (dam releases, diversions)
- Data processing requires GIS skills not common in volunteer SAR teams

## When To Use
- First step in any riverine search — before sonar, divers, or drones
- To determine search radius based on flow rate and time elapsed
- To identify high-probability entrapment points (bends, dams, debris fields)

## Source
- USGS Water Resources: https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources
- National Weather Service river forecasts
